Source 1: The cobalt chloride equilibrium

When cobalt(II) chloride dissolves in hydrochloric acid, two cobalt complex ions exist together in a dynamic equilibrium:

[Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺(aq) + 4Cl⁻(aq)  ⇌  [CoCl₄]²⁻(aq) + 6H₂O(l)
[Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ is pink [CoCl₄]²⁻ is blue a mixture looks purple

The forward reaction is endothermic (ΔH is positive).

Source 2: Measuring colour with a colour chart

The colour of the solution shows the position of equilibrium. Pink means mostly [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺, blue means mostly [CoCl₄]²⁻ and purple means a mixture.

To record the colour as a number, hold the test tube against a white tile next to a colour chart numbered 1 (pink) to 10 (blue) and choose the closest match. A higher number means the position of equilibrium lies further to the right.

Source 3: Hazards

Concentrated hydrochloric acid (12 mol dm⁻³): corrosive; causes burns and serious eye damage; gives off irritating fumes, so use it in a fume cupboard or well-ventilated room.
Cobalt(II) chloride: toxic; may cause cancer if inhaled; may cause skin and breathing sensitisation; very toxic to aquatic life.
Water bath: hot water above 60 °C can scald.
